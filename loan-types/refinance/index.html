Mortgage Refinance

Refinancing replaces an existing mortgage with a new loan. A useful comparison considers the new payment, interest rate, term, closing costs, cash received or paid, and how long you expect to keep the loan.

Who this option may be for

  • Homeowners evaluating a lower rate or different loan term.
  • Borrowers seeking to change a rate structure or remove certain mortgage costs.
  • Homeowners considering cash-out while understanding the effect on balance and equity.

Qualification basics

  • Identify the goal before comparing offers: payment, total interest, term, stability or equity access.
  • Review the Loan Estimate, points, lender credits, third-party fees and prepaid items.
  • Calculate the break-even period for upfront costs and compare total cost over the expected holding period.
  • Confirm appraisal, title, income and asset documentation requirements.

Potential benefits

  • Can change payment, term or rate structure.
  • May consolidate certain debts or access equity when appropriate.
  • Offers a chance to compare current loan structure with new alternatives.

Important considerations

  • Closing costs can reduce or delay savings.
  • Restarting or extending a term may increase total interest even if the payment falls.
  • Cash-out increases the amount secured by the home and reduces equity.

Documents to prepare

  • Current mortgage statement
  • Homeowners insurance and tax information
  • Income and employment records
  • Asset and debt statements
  • Property and occupancy details

Frequently asked questions

How do I know whether refinancing saves money?

Compare upfront costs with monthly and long-term savings, then consider how long you expect to keep the new loan.

Does a lower monthly payment always mean a better refinance?

No. A lower payment may come from extending the term, which can increase total interest. Compare both monthly and lifetime cost.

What is a cash-out refinance?

It is a refinance where the new loan is larger than the amount needed to pay off the existing mortgage and eligible costs, with the difference provided as cash, subject to program rules.

The Fixed Rate Home Equity Line is an open-end product where the full loan amount minus the origination fee will be 100% drawn at the time of origination. The initial amount funded at origination will be based on a fixed rate; however, this product contains an additional draw feature. As the borrower repays the balance on the line, the borrower may make additional draws during the draw period. If the borrower elects to make an additional draw, the interest rate for that draw will be set as of the date of the draw and will be based on an Index, which is the Prime Rate published in the Wall Street Journal for the calendar month preceding the date of the additional draw, plus a fixed margin. Accordingly, the fixed rate for any additional draw may be higher than the fixed rate for the initial draw.
