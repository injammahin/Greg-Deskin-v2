Investment & Commercial Financing

Investment and commercial real estate financing can be structured differently from owner-occupied consumer mortgages. The property’s income, borrower strength, entity structure and business purpose may all influence underwriting.

Who this option may be for

  • Real estate investors purchasing or refinancing rental property.
  • Borrowers evaluating DSCR, bank-statement, asset-based or conventional investment paths.
  • Businesses or sponsors financing commercial or larger multi-family properties.

Qualification basics

  • Clarify property type, occupancy, ownership entity and business purpose.
  • Prepare rent rolls, leases, operating statements and property expense information when applicable.
  • Underwriting may review debt-service coverage, borrower liquidity, experience and reserves.
  • Terms may include prepayment provisions, balloons, recourse or other commercial features that require careful review.

Potential benefits

  • Specialised structures can align financing with property income and investment strategy.
  • Multiple documentation paths may exist depending on programme and lender.
  • Can support single-family rentals, multi-family and commercial assets.

Important considerations

  • Rates, fees, down payments and reserves may be higher than owner-occupied financing.
  • Commercial terms can be more complex and less standardised.
  • Vacancy, repairs, market changes and property management affect risk and cash flow.

Documents to prepare

  • Entity and ownership documents
  • Rent roll and leases
  • Operating statements and tax returns
  • Property schedule and mortgage statements
  • Liquidity, reserves and experience documentation

Frequently asked questions

What is DSCR financing?

Debt-service coverage ratio programmes evaluate whether property income supports the proposed debt payment, alongside other lender requirements.

Is an investment property loan a consumer mortgage?

Some one-to-four unit investment loans use residential mortgage programmes, while business-purpose and commercial loans can follow different rules.

What should I compare besides the interest rate?

Review fees, amortisation, term, recourse, prepayment provisions, reserve requirements, renewal risk and total cash needed.

The Fixed Rate Home Equity Line is an open-end product where the full loan amount minus the origination fee will be 100% drawn at the time of origination. The initial amount funded at origination will be based on a fixed rate; however, this product contains an additional draw feature. As the borrower repays the balance on the line, the borrower may make additional draws during the draw period. If the borrower elects to make an additional draw, the interest rate for that draw will be set as of the date of the draw and will be based on an Index, which is the Prime Rate published in the Wall Street Journal for the calendar month preceding the date of the additional draw, plus a fixed margin. Accordingly, the fixed rate for any additional draw may be higher than the fixed rate for the initial draw.
